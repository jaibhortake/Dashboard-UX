Attribution is the whole job

Case Insights is one of five sources DX Copilot merges. Only its share is cited, re-authorized per record and reconcilable to a cohort. If the plan of action doesn't mark which line came from [G] versus a Slack thread, every guarantee in the PRD evaporates into "the copilot said so". Source tags on each claim are not decoration — they are the contract.

Five sources, five authorization models

Case Insights denies per record and reports the count. Glean and Slack have their own ACLs and no notion of a withheld-record tally. So "8 sources cited" must never be a single number across systems — each source reports its own coverage and its own denials, or the honesty of the whole answer is only as good as its weakest member.

The serial is the join

Photon knows what this array is doing; the graph knows what the fleet's cases said. The case-to-equipment-to-serial chain resolves at 100%, which is what makes the join safe — but DX Copilot performs it at answer time. Nothing in the graph knows about the bundle.

A log signature beats an alert code

The anchor is what Photon just extracted, not a category tag. That is BQ-28 — matching cases that share no alert code with yours. In this cohort 11 of 41 are filed under Software and a category filter loses them outright.

Prior fixes become an executable checklist

The graph supplies what to look for — the checks that appear in 24 of 28 corrected cases. Photon already holds the bundle, so gtsai verify runs them against it. Reading material becomes a diagnostic.

Retrieval is free here

Fired at bundle load, the 2.4s call finishes ~38s before the parse does. The latency worry that dogs a web UI evaporates in a tool that was already going to make you wait — so the flag costs nothing you notice.

Copyable means flush-left

No click-to-copy exists in a shell, so commands are emitted with no prompt characters and no leading spaces — a drag or triple-click gets exactly what pastes. --format=json for piping.

Both halves can fail, differently

At a dark site Fuse has only a hand-uploaded bundle and the graph excludes restricted-site cases at source. The answer names which half is degraded rather than quietly returning a thinner one. Try dark site.

Unlocked later What becomes possible when fleet telemetry is indexed in the corpus
First: why the copilot already reading Fuse logs is not this

DX Copilot reads one array's logs, on demand, for the case in front of you — depth of one. An indexed corpus holds every array's history, joinable to cases. The distinction is not availability, it is indexing: you cannot answer "how many arrays" by fan-out at question time — that is a batch job, not a retrieval — and precursor windows need log history aligned to case timestamps across thousands of arrays. Same tool list, different corpus.

The silent majority becomes visible

A case corpus only contains arrays whose owners complained. Telemetry contains every array that had the condition. "41 cases" could be 41 of 1,284 affected arrays — and today there is no way to know which. This is the single biggest change, and it moves every count in the product.

Precursors, so prediction replaces explanation

If the counter drifts 6–9 days before a case is opened, that window becomes a trigger. The product stops answering "what happened here" and starts answering "which arrays are about to do this" — the proactive-risk questions stop being retrospective.

Fixes verified in data, not in prose

Today "fixed in 6.9.4" is read out of close notes, so a silent upgrade is uncounted and a wrong close note is believed. Telemetry confirms the counter actually reset on all 19 upgraded arrays. BQ-21 BQ-34 stop depending on what someone typed.

Three catalog gaps close

Principle 1

Ask only for what is missing. Context the case already carries is never re-typed; gaps are flagged with the confidence they cost.

Principle 2

The answer is a workspace, not a wall of text. Verdict first, then structure the engineer can act on — bars, diffs, and commands with a copy button.

Principle 3

Evidence stays in the app. A citation opens a readable source, with pseudonyms resolved back to references the engineer recognises.

Principle 4

Say what was not seen. Withheld records, suppressed groups and inferred edges are surfaced, never silently dropped.

Principle 5

A turn is an immutable, citable unit. Everything the system derived — sources, evidence, trace — stays scoped to its turn; only what the engineer deliberately collects (the compare tray) floats above the thread.

Principle 6

Free-form is welcome, but never silently ungrounded. A typed question is matched to an agreed question where one fits, and labelled as inferred framing where none does.
